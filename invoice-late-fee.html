Late fees on a freelance invoice.

US freelancers · templates, not legal advice

A late fee is arithmetic you wrote down before the work started. It is not a mood.

US freelancers talk about late fees more than they collect them. The ones who collect them did two unromantic things: they put the fee in writing before the job, and they used a number they were willing to say out loud.

This page is how that usually looks. It is not legal advice. Afterdue is not a law firm. Some states limit late fees; some treat a sole proprietor’s invoice as a commercial account. You are responsible for what you send. If the balance is large or the relationship is already hostile, talk to a lawyer in your state.

Only if it was already agreed

Do not invent a 5% fee on the day the invoice goes quiet. Use a late fee only if the client already agreed to it — in a contract, an accepted estimate, or the notes on the original invoice.

If it was never mentioned, your options are still the follow-up sequence, a payment plan, pausing new work, or later a collections process. Adding a surprise fee is how a late invoice becomes a dispute.

Two structures people actually use

Pick one. Write it down. Do not stack both unless the writing says so.

A. Percent of the unpaid balance

fee = unpaid_balance × (percent / 100) new_total = unpaid_balance + fee

Typical freelance range in the US: 1.5% per month on the unpaid balance (often written as 18% APR), or a one-time 5% if you do not want to compound.

B. Flat fee

fee = flat_amount new_total = unpaid_balance + fee

Use a flat fee when the invoice is small and a percentage looks petty, or when you want one number you can say on a call. Example: $50 after 15 days past due.

Round to the cent. Put the fee on its own line on a revised invoice. Do not silently change the original line items.

When not to add a fee

  • You never mentioned a late fee before the work started.
  • You were late delivering, and they are paying against a disputed scope.
  • The amount is under about $200 and the relationship is worth more than the fee.
  • You are about to go to small claims — the court will want a clean original invoice.

Language that belongs on the invoice before the job

Invoices are due Net 15. Balances more than 15 days past due accrue a one-time late fee of 5% of the invoice total, or $50, whichever is greater.

Or, monthly:

Invoices are due Net 15. Unpaid balances accrue a finance charge of 1.5% per month (18% APR) on the unpaid amount, beginning on the 16th day after the due date.

Pause is often more useful than the fee

A $50 fee on a $400 invoice is a gesture. Pausing new requests until the balance clears is how you stop digging. Only say you will pause if you will. The follow-up email that announces a fee should attach the revised PDF and, if you use it, say that new work is paused.
