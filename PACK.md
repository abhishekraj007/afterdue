## How to use the sequence

1. Send the invoice the day you finish, or the day the milestone is accepted. Due date on the invoice. Payment instructions on the invoice. Do not hide the total in the email body only.
2. **Message 1 — Friendly** on the due date, or the next morning if they are West Coast and you sent it late.
3. **Message 2 — Still open** four calendar days later if there is no payment and no reply.
4. **Message 3 — Firm** five calendar days after that. Name a new date. Mention the late fee only if it is already in the agreement or invoice notes.
5. **Message 4 — Late fee** when that date passes. Attach a revised invoice. Update the number (e.g. 1042-A) or add a line: “Late fee — 5% of original balance.”
6. **Message 5 — Last notice** six calendar days after the late-fee invoice. Then stop reminding. Either you collect, pause work, or hand it to a collections process you have actually decided to use.

Skip a step if they reply. If they ask for a payment plan, write the plan in one email: amounts, dates, what happens if a date is missed. Do not keep “just checking in.”

Do not send all five in one week. That reads as panic, not process.

## Deposit invoice

Use this *before* the next job starts. Do not invent a deposit on work you already delivered.

**When to use it:** new client, project over about $1,500, or anyone who has paid you late once.

**Default split:** 50% due before work starts. 50% due on delivery (or on an agreed milestone). Net 7 on the deposit, Net 15 on the remainder — or due on receipt for the deposit.

**What to put on the invoice**

- Invoice number, same series (e.g. 1043).
- Line 1: `[Project] — deposit (50%)` · Qty 1 · Rate = half the project fee.
- Notes:

> This invoice is a start-work deposit for `[project]`. Work begins after this payment clears. The remaining `[remainder]` will be invoiced on `[milestone / delivery]` and is due Net 15. Deposit is applied to the project total. Deposit is non-refundable once work has started, except if I cancel the project.

**Email that goes with it** (not part of the chase sequence)

**Subject:** Deposit invoice `[invoice]` — `[project]`

Hi `[Name]`,

To lock the dates we discussed, deposit invoice `#[invoice]` is attached: `[deposit amount]`, due `[deposit due]`.

I start when it clears. The rest (`[remainder]`) invoices on `[milestone]`.

`[you]`

**If they ask to skip the deposit**

You can reduce it to 30% once. You should not skip it for a new client on a large job because they “usually pay on the back end.” That is how invoice 1042 becomes a chase sequence.

## Late-fee calculator

This is arithmetic, not a penalty you invent after the fact. Use a late fee only if the client already agreed to it — in a contract, an estimate, or the notes on the original invoice.

### Two common structures (pick one, write it down)

**A. Percent of the unpaid balance**

```
fee        = unpaid_balance × (percent / 100)
new_total  = unpaid_balance + fee
```

Typical freelance range in the US: **1.5% per month** on the unpaid balance, or a **one-time 5%** if you do not want to compound. Do not stack both unless the contract says so.

**B. Flat fee**

```
fee        = flat_amount
new_total  = unpaid_balance + fee
```

Use a flat fee when the invoice is small and a percentage looks petty, or when you want one number you can say out loud. Example: $50 after 15 days past due.

### Worked example (matches the site sample)

Honey & Rye Bakery · Invoice 1042

| | |
|---|---|
| Subtotal | $3,960.00 |
| Tax 8.25% | $326.70 |
| Original total | $4,286.70 |
| Days past due (in this example) | 15 |
| Fee type | one-time 5% of original total |
| Late fee | $214.34 |
| **New total** | **$4,501.04** |

If you use 1.5% per month instead:

```
fee       = 4286.70 × 0.015 = 64.30
new_total = 4351.00
```

Round to the cent. Put the fee on its own line. Do not silently change the original line items.

### When not to add a fee

- You never mentioned a late fee before the work started.
- You were late delivering, and they are paying against a disputed scope.
- The amount is under ~$200 and the relationship is worth more than the fee.
- You are about to go to small claims — the court will want a clean original invoice.

### One-line you can paste into invoice notes (before the job)

> Invoices are due Net 15. Balances more than 15 days past due accrue a one-time late fee of 5% of the invoice total, or $50, whichever is greater.

Or, monthly:

> Invoices are due Net 15. Unpaid balances accrue a finance charge of 1.5% per month (18% APR) on the unpaid amount, beginning on the 16th day after the due date.

## Contract clauses

Templates. Edit the brackets. Have counsel review if the engagement is large, recurring, or in a state with strict consumer-fee rules. Some states limit late fees; some treat sole-proprietor work as a commercial account. You are responsible for what you send.

Use these in a one-page agreement, an estimate that the client accepts in writing, or the “Terms” block of a proposal. Put the same numbers on the invoice.

### Net-15 payment

**Payment.** Client will pay invoices in US dollars within fifteen (15) days of the invoice date (“Net 15”). Accepted methods: `[Zelle / ACH / Venmo / other]`. Client will include the invoice number in the payment memo. Work product may be delivered as scheduled; transfer of final files or usage rights may be withheld until the related invoice is paid in full.

### Late fee

**Late amounts.** Any amount not received by the due date is past due. Beginning on the sixteenth (16th) day after the due date, Designer/Contractor may add a one-time late fee equal to five percent (5%) of the past-due invoice total, or fifty dollars ($50), whichever is greater. Designer/Contractor may issue a revised invoice for the new total. Designer/Contractor is not required to start additional work, including revisions outside the original scope, while any invoice is past due.

*Alternate (monthly finance charge):*

**Late amounts.** Past-due balances accrue a finance charge of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month (eighteen percent (18%) APR) on the unpaid amount, calculated from the day after the due date until paid. Designer/Contractor may issue a revised invoice each month the balance remains unpaid.

### Deposit (pair with the deposit invoice)

**Deposit.** A non-refundable deposit of fifty percent (50%) of the project fee is due before work starts. Work start date is the later of the agreed start date and clearance of the deposit. The remaining fifty percent (50%) is due Net 15 from the invoice issued on `[delivery / named milestone]`. If Client cancels after work has started, the deposit is retained as liquidated compensation for reserved time, and any work already delivered remains payable.

### Pause of work

**Pause.** If any invoice is more than fifteen (15) days past due, Designer/Contractor may pause work, including scheduled revisions and new requests, until the past-due amount (and any late fee then owing) is paid. Pause days do not count against delivery dates. Designer/Contractor will resume within `[3]` business days of cleared payment.

### Last notice / collections (keep short)

**Collection.** If a past-due invoice remains unpaid `[21]` days after a written last notice, Designer/Contractor may refer the amount to a collections process or pursue available remedies, and may recover reasonable costs of collection to the extent allowed by law. This clause is not a threat of criminal action and is not a credit report filing by itself.
